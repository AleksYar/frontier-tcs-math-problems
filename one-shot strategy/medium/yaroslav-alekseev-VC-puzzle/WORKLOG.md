- **Re-established contradiction setup.** Assume every `H in mathcal H` has `|H| <= n/2`. Then every three coordinate columns of the incidence matrix contain trace `111`, but no three columns contain all eight traces. Equivalently, with `F={ [n]\\H:H in mathcal H}`, every `F` has size at least `n/2`, `VCdim(F)<=2`, and no three points form a transversal of `F`.
- **Re-established dual formulation.** For coordinate `i`, let `A_i={H in mathcal H:i in H}` on the row universe. The `A_i` are 3-wise intersecting; no three of them are independent (have all eight Venn cells); and a universe point corresponding to row `H` belongs to exactly `|H|` of them. The theorem is therefore the sharp majority assertion that such a dual family has a point in more than half of its `n` indexed sets.
- **Strong-induction reduction (verified).** A least counterexample cannot have odd order: restriction to any `n-1` coordinates and induction would give an original row of weight at least `(n+1)/2`. Hence a least counterexample has `n=2m`. Restriction after deleting one coordinate gives, for every `x`, an `m`-row avoiding `x`; restriction after deleting a pair gives, for every `x,y`, an `m`-row avoiding both. Deleting any triple gives a row with at least `m-1` ones outside that triple, hence a trace of weight at most one on the deleted triple. Consequently, if `000` is absent on a triple, all three singleton traces occur (use the three pair-avoiding `m`-rows).
- **Pair-cover lemma (verified).** If a family on `N>=2` covers every pair and has VC dimension at most one, it contains a row of weight `>N/2`. The proof is by the same least-counterexample induction: odd `N` reduces immediately; for `N=2r`, induction produces an `r`-row avoiding each prescribed point/pair. Choose an `r`-row `A`, two outside points covered together by `B`, and `a in A\\B`; then `A,B`, a pair-covering row, and a pair-avoiding row realize `10,01,11,00` on a suitable cross pair.
- **Link rigidity (verified).** In a least `2m`-counterexample, for every coordinate `i`, the upper link `L_i={H\\{i}:i in H}` pair-covers the other `2m-1` coordinates, all its rows have size at most `m-1`, and the pair-cover lemma forces `VCdim(L_i)=2`. Thus some pair is shattered in every upper link.
- **Maximum-row cross-biclique (verified).** Fix an `m`-row `A`, put `B=[2m]\\A`, and apply induction to the restriction on `B`. There is a row `R` with `E=R cap B`, `|E|>m/2`. If `D=A\\R`, then maximality gives `|D|>=|E|>m/2`. Every cross pair `{d,e}` with `d in D,e in E` is shattered: `A,R`, a triple-covering row, and an `m`-row avoiding the pair give `10,01,11,00`.
- **Useful saturation reformulation (verified).** For each triple choose one trace absent from `H`; it is never `111`. Enlarging `H` to all low-weight vectors (weight at most `m`) which avoid all chosen forbidden traces preserves VC dimension at most two and preserves the original `111` witnesses. Thus a counterexample is equivalent to a ternary-constraint system (one forbidden non-`111` pattern per triple) having, for every triple, a low-weight satisfying assignment equal to `111` there.
- **Discarded strengthening: empty row forces co-size at most two (false).** On a ground set partitioned into four nonempty blocks, the family consisting of the empty set and the complements of the four blocks is triple-covering and has VC dimension two. For four equal blocks its largest row has density `3/4`, not co-size at most two. The earlier matching-pair construction is the special equal-block example with blocks of size two.
- **Weighted/cloning route (partially verified, incomplete).** A least counterexample implies a weighted-majority statement for every nonnegative integer coordinate weighting of total mass `<n`, by replacing coordinates with that many identical copies and applying induction. This recovers the maximum rows avoiding singletons/pairs and supplies further perturbed inequalities. Extending this to arbitrary weights would solve the problem by minimax, but minimality in the number of coordinates does not justify clone totals `>=n`; that extension is currently unproved.
- **Literature check.** The general covering-family/Sauer counting bound in Peterzil--Steinmeyer only handles `n` much larger than the block size and gives no result at `n=2s`. Generic epsilon-net and fractional-transversal bounds likewise do not give the exact size-three, density-one-half assertion needed here.
- **Further verified criticality.** In a least counterexample, cloning coordinates with nonnegative integer multiplicities of total `<n` and applying induction proves weighted majority for every such multiplicity vector. In particular, deleting one or two unit weights forces maximum half-sized rows avoiding every prescribed singleton or pair. This does *not* extend to arbitrary weights because a cloning denominator may exceed `n`.
- **Minimal-transversal/private-row formulation.** For the complement family `F`, a minimum transversal `X` has size `t>=4`. Minimality supplies private edges `F_i` with `F_i cap X={i}`. Equivalently the original family contains rows whose traces on `X` are all co-singletons. Hence every triple of `X` already has its three doubleton traces and `111`. Density gives `t<=floor(n/2)+1`; in the even uniform boundary case, writing `Y=[n]\X`, each private complement is `{i} union (Y\B_i)` with `|B_i|=m+1-t`.
- **Down-compression endpoint (verified but insufficient).** Add the empty complement (corresponding to the full original row) formally and apply standard one-coordinate down-compressions. They preserve VC dimension and preserve the property that every triple has a *nonempty* disjoint complement witness: a singleton `{i}` cannot be shifted to empty because empty is already present. The fully compressed family is downward closed and, under VC dimension two, consists only of labels of size at most two. This gives an injective compression label contained in each original complement, but it loses the lower bound on complement sizes and by itself gives no contradiction.
- **False strengthening/counterexamples.** An empty row plus triple coverage and VC dimension two does not force co-size at most two: partition the ground set into four nonempty blocks and take the empty set together with the four block complements. The stronger claim that the maximum is always at least `n-2` is also false (projective-plane complement examples), and matching-pair complements refute a related `q-1` claim.
- **Cross-biclique refinement.** For a maximum `m`-row `A` in a least even counterexample, induction on its complement gives a row `R`. With `D=A\R` and `E=R\A`, one has `|D|=|E|>m/2`; every cross pair in `D times E` is shattered. This large complete bipartite graph in the shattering graph is not alone contradictory (the class of all sets of size at most two has all pairs shattered).
- **Algebraic/ordinary shifting audit.** A fully left-compressed low-weight triple-cover would shatter the final three coordinates: from a member containing them, shift each unwanted final coordinate to a distinct unused earlier coordinate while keeping the wanted trace; the half-size bound supplies enough unused coordinates. However ordinary size-preserving `(i,j)`-shifting does **not** preserve the complete 3-shadow, even for a uniform half-sized family. An explicit computer-checked `n=8,k=4` example lost a covered triple after the shift. Therefore no proof may assume shadow preservation. Exterior algebraic shifting preserves shadows in standard settings, but preservation of VC dimension was not established and is not being assumed.
- **Push-up uniformization attempt (later falsified in the adversarial audit).** The capped coordinate push-up replaces a lone lower set `A` (`i notin A`, `|A|<m`) by `A union {i}`. It preserves triple coverage, and an initially overlooked argument claimed it preserved VC dimension. That claim is false: a lower witness of maximum allowed size `m` can survive without having an upper mate. Explicitly, on four points with cap two, `F={empty,{2},{0,1},{0}}` has VC dimension one, while pushing coordinate `2` gives `{empty,{2},{0,1},{0,2}}`, which has VC dimension two. Thus the asserted reduction of every counterexample to an `m`-uniform one is **withdrawn**.
- **Conditional uniform core.** It remains independently true and useful to study the lemma that every `k`-uniform family on `n>=2k` covering all triples should have VC dimension at least three, and induction reduces that lemma to `n=2k`. But because push-up uniformization is invalid, proving this lemma alone would no longer prove the original nonuniform theorem. All subsequent statements explicitly set in the uniform boundary (private triples, complementary halves, minimum transversals, and exact searches) are conditional structural progress for that subproblem, not reductions of the original theorem.
- **Discarded uniform-shift claim (explicit falsification).** A random/exhaustive small search found a uniform half-sized 3-cover on `n=8,k=4` for which an ordinary `(1,2)`-shift loses a triple; as expected this example has VC dimension at least three, but that fact does not make shadow preservation available without a separate proof.
- **Complete rooted-triple closure lemma (verified).** Suppose one assigns to every triple `{a,b,c}` one implication, one of `ab=>c`, `ac=>b`, `bc=>a`, and closes sets under all implications. Some pair generates the entire ground set. Proof: choose a pair `ab` whose closure `C` has maximum size. If `x` is outside `C`, the rule on `abx` cannot be `ab=>x`; if it is `ax=>b`, then the closure of `ax` contains `ab`, hence all of `C`, as well as `x`, contradicting maximality; the case `bx=>a` is symmetric. Exhaustive checks through five vertices independently confirmed the lemma before the one-line maximal-closure proof was accepted.
- **Application limit of the closure lemma.** If a trace family contains the empty pattern, all singleton patterns, and `111` on every triple while having VC dimension two, every triple has a missing doubleton and hence defines such an implication. The lemma then forces a pair whose every containing row is the full ground set. This is immediately contradictory if the full row is absent but pairs are covered. In the actual minimum-transversal trace on `X`, the empty and singleton patterns are available locally and every pair is covered, but `111` need not occur: when it is absent, pair coverage instead supplies all three doubletons. Thus the closure lemma alone does not close the main case; the trace family `binom(X,<=2)` is the model obstruction.
- **Extreme minimum-transversal case eliminated (verified).** In the even uniform core let complements be `m`-uniform on `2m` and let a minimum transversal `X` have size `t`. If `t=m+1`, write `Y=[2m]\X`, so `|Y|=m-1`. Private edges give `000` and all singleton traces on every triple of `X`. For a desired pair `P` in such a triple, the `(t-1)`-set `(X\P) union {y}` is not a transversal; an edge disjoint from it must use both points of `P`, since only `m-2` outside points remain. For `111`, use `(X\T) union {y,z}`; only `m-3` outside points remain, forcing all three points of `T`. Hence every triple of `X` is shattered, eliminating `t=m+1`.
- **Near-extreme structure.** If `t=m`, then `|Y|=m` and every private complement has the exact form `{x_i} union (Y\{phi(i)})` for a map `phi:X->Y`. This sharp structure is the current boundary subcase; exchange with a `(t-1)`-set forces one, rather than both, members of a desired pair, so the preceding argument loses exactly one unit and does not extend directly.
- **Complementary-half closure lemma (verified, conditional progress).** If the even uniform family contains complementary blocks `A,B`, then for every `x in A` there are `y,z in B` such that the family realizes both exact doubleton traces `xy` and `xz` on `{x,y,z}`. Otherwise, for every pair `y,z` orient at least one direction `y->z` meaning every row containing `x,y` also contains `z`. A tournament has a vertex reaching all others; implication closure would then force every row containing the corresponding cross pair to contain `{x} union B`, of size `m+1`, impossible. The same argument applies to the complement family because deletion induction makes it pair-covering, and hence supplies two singleton traces in the original family (possibly for a different pair `y,z`). Synchronizing the two selected pairs and forcing `000` remain unresolved.
- **Exact small-case falsification search.** A complete branch search for `n=8,k=4` (fixing one block by symmetry) explored 380,357 nodes and confirmed that no uniform half-sized VC-two family covers all 56 triples; the largest 3-shadow encountered had size 36. This is supporting evidence only, not a proof for general `m`, and no numerical pattern from the extremizer was promoted to a theorem.
- **Other routes audited and not completed.** (i) Polynomial missing-pattern identities reduce each cubic monomial on the class to a quadratic polynomial (`000`, singleton, and doubleton forbidden patterns give three explicit forms), but summing them has no controlled sign on all half-weight rows. (ii) The simplicial-complex formulation gives a complex with complete 2-skeleton and facets of size at most half; neither ordinary algebraic shifting nor a Leray bound was shown to preserve the facet-family VC dimension. (iii) Generic epsilon-net, fractional-transversal, and packing bounds are too weak at `epsilon=1/2`; projective planes explicitly show why pairwise intersection plus VC dimension two alone cannot bound the transversal number. (iv) Sample-compression/teaching-dimension language restates the desired conclusion as a 3-coordinate teaching set for the adjoined all-one concept, but general VC-two classes can give that concept arbitrarily large teaching dimension when the distance hypothesis is removed. (v) The ternary CSP formulation has one 1-valid forbidden pattern per triple; Horn/dual-Horn closure works only for uniform forbidden-pattern types, and no Ramsey reduction with usable exact constants was obtained.
- **Adversarial correction of a deletion arithmetic claim.** Deleting a triple from `n=2m` and applying induction gives at least `m-1` ones outside, not `m`; it therefore yields a trace of weight at most one, but does not force `000`. Several closure attempts fail precisely because `000` is not guaranteed.
- **Small-transversal induction (verified late reduction).** In the uniform boundary on `2m`, the original family itself cannot have a transversal of size one or two. Indeed, deleting such a transversal `T` leaves a triple-covering restriction on `2m-|T|` points, every trace having size at most `m-1`; induction demands a trace larger than half, impossible for `|T|<=2`. Therefore a triple with missing `000` must be a *minimal* 3-transversal. In that last case its three private rows give the singleton traces, `111` is present, and at least one doubleton is absent; if `xy` is absent then every row containing `x,y` contains the third point, and the rows containing the whole transversal have outside traces covering all remaining points. This exact `tau=3` equality case remains unresolved.
- **Criticality inside the `tau=3` case.** If `T` is a minimal 3-transversal and `U=[2m]\T`, then for every `u in U`, deleting `T union {u}` and applying induction produces an original row meeting `T` in exactly one point and meeting `U` in exactly `m-1` points while avoiding `u`. Thus the maximal outside traces (colored by which private point of `T` they use) collectively avoid every outside coordinate. This is stronger than mere existence of the three private rows, but a proof that one color class has enough avoidance/covering structure to shatter a triple was not obtained.
- **Shift-loss obstruction (verified, but the route ultimately fails at VC preservation).** In a least uniform counterexample on `2m` (`m>=4`), no four-set `Q` can meet every block in at least two points: otherwise restriction to the `2m-4` outside points still covers all triples, all traces have size at most `m-2`, and induction gives a trace larger than `(2m-4)/2=m-2`. If an ordinary `(i,j)`-shift loses coverage of a triple `{j,a,b}`, triple coverage of `{i,a,b}`, `{i,j,a}`, and `{i,j,b}` supplies on `Q={i,j,a,b}` all four co-singleton traces. Each three-subset of `Q` therefore has `111` and all three doubletons; VC dimension two forces `000` to be absent there. Hence every block meets every three-subset of `Q`, so meets `Q` at least twice, contradicting the preceding observation. Thus, *conditional on retaining VC dimension two*, shifts cannot lose triple coverage.
- **Crucial falsification: ordinary shifts can raise VC dimension, even for uniform families.** Exhaustive enumeration found the 2-uniform family `{{0,1},{1,2},{0,4},{0,3}}` on five points, of VC dimension one, whose `(2,3)`-shift (zero-based coordinates) is `{{0,1},{0,2},{0,4},{1,2}}` and has VC dimension two. A smaller nonuniform example on three points raises VC dimension one to two as well. Therefore the attractive plan “shift to a fully shifted uniform triple-cover, then shatter the final triple” is invalid unless one separately proves that any VC-raising shift is impossible under the triple-cover hypotheses. That missing assertion was not established. The terminal part itself is valid: a fully shifted `m`-uniform triple-cover on `2m` shatters the last three points, by replacing any unwanted last coordinate of a block covering them with distinct smaller unused coordinates.
- **Most promising route after the uniformization audit: saturated one-step extensions.** Enlarge a putative low-weight counterexample by every row of weight at most `m` whose addition does not create a shattered triple. If `A` is a row of size `<m` and `i notin A` but `A+i` is absent, maximality gives a certificate triple `S` which would be shattered by adding `A+i`; hence the old family realizes exactly the other seven traces. Necessarily `i in S`, and since `111` was already present, `(A+i)|S` is either the singleton `i` or a doubleton `ia` (it cannot be `111`). In the doubleton case the seven-pattern certificate gives the Horn implication `ia=>b`; in the singleton case it gives the weaker disjunction “every row containing `i` contains `a` or `b`.” A proof would follow if these certificates for all blocked extensions of one low row could be combined, via the verified rooted-closure lemma or a counting argument, to force more than `m` coordinates in a row. The unresolved difficulty is controlling the singleton/disjunctive certificates and making the locally chosen companion coordinates coherent.
