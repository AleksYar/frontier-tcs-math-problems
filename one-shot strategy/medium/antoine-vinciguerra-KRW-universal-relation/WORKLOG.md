## 2026-08-28T12:07:03+02:00 - Exact stage lemma and the remaining fortified core

- Recast the target exactly as a generalized KW game. With `h=g^m : {0,1}^{mn}->{0,1}^m`, the relation `U_m diamond KW_g` is `KW_h`: on `h(X)!=h(Y)`, find a raw input coordinate where `X,Y` differ.
- Proved the following exact universal-stage lemma. Choose equal-cardinality nonempty sets `H_0 subset g^{-1}(0)` and `H_1 subset g^{-1}(1)`, put `H=H_0 union H_1` and `X_0=H^m`. Every fiber of `g^m` in `X_0` has the same size. If a depth-`c` protocol is followed on diagonal pairs `(Z,Z)`, then at every level `t` one partial transcript has diagonal intersection `S subset X_0` of density at least `2^{-t}`. For the relaxed game this transcript is nonterminal whenever `t<m`; for the strict-promise game it is nonterminal whenever `t<=m-2`. Consequently
  `c >= t + min_{S subset X_0, dens(S)>=2^{-t}} CC(KW_{g^m}|_{S x S})`
  (up to the stated constant convention). Proof: pad to level `t`, average over at most `2^t` nodes, and note that a reject leaf can meet only one `g^m`-fiber; under the strict promise, a coordinate-labeled leaf can meet at most two fibers.
- Proved the auxiliary range bound for every vector-valued map `h` and every domain subset `S`:
  `CC(KW_h|_{S x S}) >= log_2 |h(S)| - 1`.
  Run the protocol on all diagonal pairs. At a leaf labeled coordinate `q`, the `h`-value is constant among diagonal inputs having `q`-bit 0 and separately among those having `q`-bit 1, hence that leaf accounts for at most two range values. A depth-`d` tree has at most `2^d` leaves.
- Applying the range bound to the dense `S` in the stage lemma recovers `c>=m-O(1)` exactly, but cannot add `D(g)`: a dense `S` may project under `g^m` to a sparse error-correcting code with no adjacent output vectors. Comparing two distant fibers leads precisely to a `1-out-of-k` KW game, whose near-preservation of `D(g)` is open.
- Tested the proposed “pick a large row projection” repair. Although `|S|` forces at least one large coordinate projection by entropy, correlations inside `S` can make every fixed `(m-1)`-row fiber tiny. A local embedding of `KW_g` needs a large common fiber or safe row-wise symmetries of `g`; neither follows for arbitrary `g`.
- Extended small computational tests heuristically to all 2-bit functions and selected 3-bit functions (`AND_3`, `MUX_1`). Natural-predicate synthesis found the two-stage depths (e.g. depth 4 for `m=2,g=AND_2/XOR_2/MUX_1`, depth 5 for `m=2,g=AND_3`) and no shorter protocol. ## 2026-08-28T13:04:58+02:00 - Universal-minor shortcut falsified and coding route revisited

- Formalized the proposed local reduction from `U_k` to `KW_g`.  Suppose Alice and Bob locally encode `u,v` as `alpha(u), beta(v)`, every off-diagonal pair has opposite `g`-values, and every differing encoded coordinate `j` has a fixed decoded coordinate `phi(j)` on which `u,v` differ.  For `k>=3`, fix `j` and `r=phi(j)`.  Output preservation says `alpha(u)_j=beta(v)_j` whenever `u!=v` and `u_r=v_r`.  Since each `r`-fiber has at least four strings, chaining through a third string gives `alpha(u)_j=beta(u)_j` for every `u,j`; hence `alpha(u)=beta(u)`, contradicting their opposite `g`-values.  Thus this entire universal-minor reduction route is impossible (under the standard fixed local output decoding) for `k>=3`, not merely unavailable for some pathological `g`.
- Audited the small exception rather than overgeneralizing: for `k=2` the chaining argument fails, and there is in fact an encoding into `KW_PARITY_3`.  Let `alpha(u1,u2)=(u2,u1,u1 xor u2)` and `beta(v1,v2)=(not v2,not v1,not(v1 xor v2))`.  The images have opposite 3-bit parity.  A difference in encoded coordinates 1, 2, or 3 certifies respectively a difference in original bit 1, bit 2, or (for unequal inputs of equal parity) either bit.  This verifies that the impossibility threshold really is `k>=3`.
- Re-derived why an outer error-correcting-code restriction leads to the `1-out-of-k` relation: a code of distance `k` can retain `m-O(k log(m/k))` bits of outer entropy, but every surviving pair of outer values may differ in `k` rows.  Recovering the desired bound then needs the distinct-row `1-out-of-k` game to retain `D(g)-o(D(g))`.  Repetition would make the reduction from one copy trivial, but the distinctness requirement is exactly what prevents duplicating the unknown KW input.  No general simulation better than the direct-sum-type open problem was obtained.

  `CC(U_m diamond KW_g) >= m+D-2 epsilon r-O(1)`.
  This is a fully proved conditional reduction of the requested inequality to a quantitatively fortified hard core; its unproved premise is a balanced quantitative form of the known open core statement (and isolates the same row-selection obstruction).
- Compared this premise with Dinur--Meir's proved fortification lemma for a single KW rectangle.  Their lemma fortifies *protocol size* under one-sided vertex restrictions while retaining a constant fraction of minimum leaf count.  Taking logarithms controls only `log L(g)`, which can be a constant factor below `D(g)`, and it does not handle the correlated choice among many rows.  Therefore it cannot be substituted into the conditional theorem without both a depth-preserving fortification result and a `1-out-of-k`/row-selection theorem.
- Tried a correlated core in which every matrix has one globally hard row and all other rows are canonical representatives of its outer bits. Replicating the hard input in all differing rows would repair this only if arbitrary `g` supplied local fiber-preserving transformations, which it need not.  This route therefore reduces once more to the same row-selection obstruction.

## 2026-08-28T13:09:35+02:00 - Diagonal entropy identity and deterministic-depth barrier

- For the relaxed relation, proved an exact information identity.  Run any correct protocol `Pi` on diagonal inputs `(Z,Z)`.  Its output must be rejection.  If two diagonal inputs with different `g^m`-values had the same rejecting transcript, their cross pair would be a valid unequal-output input on which that leaf rejects, a contradiction.  Thus `g^m(Z)` is a deterministic function of the transcript `T=Pi(Z,Z)`.  Under any diagonal distribution for which `g^m(Z)` is uniform, `H(T)=m+H(T | g^m(Z))`.
- This shows that messages revealing inner representatives during the outer stage already add conditional transcript entropy; a genuinely additive proof would follow if low `H(T | g^m(Z))` guaranteed a residual KW game of depth `D(g)-o(D(g))`.  It does not follow from deterministic depth: non-monotone KW relations have the known randomized-protocol/hard-distribution barrier, so worst-case `D(g)` cannot in general be certified by one protocol-independent information distribution.  Allowing the distribution to depend on the candidate protocol leads back to the recursive-rank preference conflict recorded above.
- Rechecked the stage and range lemmas adversarially.  Their use of diagonal inputs is valid even for the strict promise because a deterministic protocol tree's local message functions define paths off-promise; if `z,z'` lie in the diagonal intersection of one transcript rectangle, both cross pairs lie in that rectangle.  At a coordinate leaf, three distinct `g^m`-values would put two representatives with the same output-coordinate bit and make that cross pair invalid for the leaf, so at most two values occur.  In the relaxed game, a rejecting leaf contains only one value.  No promise assumption is silently used beyond correctness on cross pairs.
- Proved a small balancing lemma for the starting inner rectangle.  If the two `g`-fibers have sizes `a,b`, put `s=min{a,b}` and `q=ceil(max{a,b}/s)`; partition the larger fiber into `q` pieces and pad each to size `s`.  Otherwise the player on the larger side could send a piece index and run the best equal-cardinality restricted protocol.  Hence some equal-cardinality subrelation has depth at least `D(g)-ceil(log_2 q)`.  This handles fiber-size imbalance but supplies no protection against the subsequent correlated dense restriction, so it does not repair Conjecture 9.4.
- Verified a general deterministic partition tradeoff: for partitions `A=union A_p`, `B=union B_q`, some cell rectangle retains depth at least `D(AxB)-ceil(log P)-ceil(log Q)`, since otherwise the parties send their cell indices and run the corresponding protocol.  Applying this to partitions induced by diagonal transcripts says that either many inner transcript cells are exposed or a hard cell pair remains.  The attempted composition still loses twice the cell-index information and, more seriously, selects different hard cell pairs/rows in different outer contexts.  Thus it reproduces the rank-preference obstruction rather than resolving it.
- Generalized the stage lemma to weighted cores.  Choose arbitrary distributions on each `g`-fiber, make the outer bit uniform, and take the row-product distribution.  Then `g^m(Z)` is exactly uniform, averaging gives a diagonal transcript event of mass `2^{-t}`, and terminal diagonal events have mass at most `2^{-m}` (relaxed reject) or `2^{1-m}` (strict coordinate leaf).  The conditional fortification theorem therefore works with probability mass instead of equal-cardinality fibers.  This removes fiber-size imbalance entirely; the unresolved requirement is still that every sufficiently heavy correlated event retain almost all deterministic KW depth.
- Reformulated the final obstruction in the relaxed formulation as a colored-tree alignment problem.  Let `K_a` be the number of rejecting protocol leaves hit by diagonal inputs of outer value `a`; these leaves partition each fiber.  For adjacent outer values `a,b`, fixing other rows canonically turns the two leaf partitions into partitions of one `KW_g`, so the deterministic partition tradeoff selects a hard cell pair.  If the two corresponding reject leaves shared an `m-o(r)`-long prefix, its suffix would prove the target.  Hypercube/tree counting can force some adjacent leaves to share deep structure, but it does not force the pair selected for residual KW hardness to be that pair.  This precisely exposes how a protocol may place easy cell pairs deep and hard cell pairs shallow.
