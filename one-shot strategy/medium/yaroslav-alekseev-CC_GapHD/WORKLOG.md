- **Initial sanity check** — The lower bound cannot follow from the promised inputs alone: public sampling of `O(1)` coordinates solves the wide-gap *partial* problem. Thus a valid proof must crucially use the protocol's required correctness on middle-distance inputs.
- **Attempt 1: diagonal/far fooling rectangles** — A deterministic `0`-rectangle meeting the diagonal in a set `S` cannot contain two elements of `S` at mutual distance at least `2n/3`; anticode bounds would give a linear deterministic lower bound. This route does **not** directly yield the randomized result: the partial problem's coordinate-sampling protocol is an explicit counterexample to any purported hard distribution supported only on close/far promised pairs.
- **Attempt 2: symmetrization by translations/permutations** — Averaging an arbitrary completion over cube automorphisms produces a distance-dependent acceptance profile fixed at `0` on distances `<=n/3` and `1` on distances `>=2n/3`. Merely locating a transition is insufficient: the averaged profile can change by only `O(1/n)` per layer, and estimating that small a gap would destroy a linear reduction. No lemma claimed from this route yet.

## Checkpoint — Unix 1787864750

- **Literature cross-check (current as of 2026-08-27 search)** — The exact assertion is Conjecture 2 in Göös–Harms–Riazanov–Sofronova–Sokolov–Yuan, *Pseudodeterministic Communication Complexity* (ECCC TR25-193 / STOC 2026). That paper states the best then-current lower bound for this exact `GapHD` completion problem was `Omega(log log n)` (Blondal–Hatami–Hatami–Lalov–Tretiak). This is evidence that the requested linear statement is a research-level open conjecture, not a standard exercise; it is not itself a proof that no solution now exists, so proof search continues.
- **Verified obstruction to a naive reduction** — For an XOR-invariant completion `f(x,y)=h(x xor y)`, choosing a maximum-weight `0` input of `h` yields an `OR` restriction in query complexity, but its XOR lift is `OR(x xor y)=NEQ(x,y)`, which has constant public-coin communication. Thus the elementary maximal-weight proof giving `Omega(n)` randomized *query* complexity cannot be lifted through the one-bit XOR gadget.
- **Verified normalization lemma (potential structural reduction)** — From any completion `f` of cost `C`, one can construct, at cost `O(C)+O(1)`, another completion `F` satisfying `F(x,y)=F(y,x)`, `F(bar(x),y)=1-F(x,y)`, and `F(x,bar(y))=1-F(x,y)`. For an involution, evaluate the function at both orbit points; if the values differ, retain the oriented value, and if they agree, use the tie-breaker `x_1 xor y_1` (or `y_1` in the first one-sided antipodalization). Independent constant-error amplification makes the composed protocol bounded-error. Algebraic case checking confirms the invariances and the promise is preserved because close/far paired inputs already have opposite forced values. This does not give translation/permutation invariance.
- **Attempt 3: force a symmetric distance threshold** — Antipodalization gives layer densities `q_{n-d}=1-q_d`, but this alone is insufficient. The cheap function `x_1 xor y_1` is symmetric and antipodal and has layer density `q_d=d/n`, demonstrating that antipodality permits a gradual transition of averaged labels. The ball constraints must therefore be used beyond the averaged profile.
- **Attempt 4: iterative sprinkling in subcubes** — Conditioning a random set of `k` free XOR coordinates to be `1` shifts Hamming distance upward. For `k=o(sqrt(n))`, the sprinkled and uniform distributions are close even to an unrestricted test, but an additive loss of order `k/sqrt(n)` per stage accumulates before enough coordinates are forced. Using protocol leaves restores a constant `0`-density invariant but incurs up to `C` bits/entropy deficiency per stage; making planted coordinates dominate this loss requires `C^2=o(n)`, reproducing the familiar square-root barrier rather than a linear bound. The tempting claim that one can reuse a single leaf through all stages is unsupported: a fixed public-coin seed can have its error set correlated with the adaptively selected later subcube.

- **Verified affine-leaf fact** — Every affine subspace of `F_2^n` of codimension `q` contains a vector of weight at most `q`: represent its syndrome as a sum of at most `q` linearly independent columns of a parity-check matrix. Applying this to the complemented coset shows it also contains a vector of weight at least `n-q`. Hence every leaf of a deterministic parity decision tree of depth `< n/3` contains both a forced `0` and a forced `1`. This proves the deterministic parity lower bound, but not the randomized bound: one erroneous witness per leaf may have exponentially tiny mass, and random parity hashing solves an individual `OR` star in constant queries.
- **Attempt 7: maximal-zero covering family** — Inclusion-maximal `0` supports all have size `<2n/3` and collectively cover every support of size `<=n/3`; elementary covering-number estimates force exponentially many such maximal zeros. The hoped-for claim that one random parity sketch cannot handle all these stars simultaneously remains unproved; pairwise collision bounds are too weak because constant-bit random hashes already keep every fixed opposite-labeled pair's collision probability below a constant.

- **Verified linear lower bounds in weaker regimes** — Let `M_n` be the largest size of a binary family of diameter `<2n/3`. By Kleitman's diameter theorem, `M_n <= 2^{H(1/3)n+o(n)}`. If a monochromatic `0` rectangle `A x B` contains diagonal inputs `(x,x)` for `x` in `S=A intersect B`, then every two members of `S` have distance `<2n/3`; hence `|S|<=M_n`. Covering all `2^n` diagonal inputs by deterministic `0` leaves requires at least `2^n/M_n=2^{(1-H(1/3))n-o(n)}` leaves. The same argument on the anti-diagonal `(x,bar(x))` applies to `1` rectangles. Therefore every completion has deterministic, `0`-nondeterministic, and `1`-nondeterministic communication complexity `Omega(n)` (and bounded-worst-cost zero-error public-coin complexity `Omega(n)`). This argument does not survive constant two-sided error, as coordinate sampling explicitly solves the partial close/far promise.
- **Attempt 8: monotone closure** — Replacing the zero set by its downward closure under inclusion produces another valid monotone completion, and its maximal-zero/minimal-one certificates are both linear-size. However, computing that closure from a protocol for the original `f` requires an existential search over supersets and is not known to preserve randomized communication or parity-query cost. Treating this as a free monotonicization would be an invalid step.
- **Parity-stage audit against Gavinsky's proof** — The published `Omega(sqrt(n))` parity-query proof plants `t_j roughly n_j/k(n_j)` one-bits per stage while intersecting with an affine leaf of codimension at most `k(n_j)`. Summing codimension loss over stages yields the inequality `k^2=Omega(n)`. A linear conclusion would require amortizing or avoiding the fresh `k`-dimensional loss at each stage. Reusing one deterministic leaf is not justified for a randomized protocol, and this is the precise square-root obstruction in that approach.
- **Attempt 9: amplify the known unbounded lower bound by padding/direct sum** — Repeating coordinates embeds a completion problem on `m=Theta(n)` bits into one on `n` bits, so it gives monotonicity only at constant-factor scales. Splitting into many blocks does not give a direct sum: the output is a single threshold of the aggregate distance, and changing one small block cannot traverse the target gap of width `n/3`. Therefore the known `Omega(log log n)` lower bound cannot be bootstrapped to `Omega(n)` by a naive padding argument.
- **Attempt 10: reduce the lifted `GapMaj o IP_m` theorem back to GapHD** — Separate local encodings of the two `IP_m` inputs whose Hamming distance had a constant relative gap according to the inner-product bit would give a constant-margin sign realization of the `IP_m` communication matrix. This contradicts its exponentially small margin/discrepancy; any realizable local distance gap therefore shrinks exponentially in `m`, and amplifying it to the constant GapHD gap incurs exponential scale. With the published choice `m=Theta(log n)`, the resulting polynomial loss cannot transfer the lifted lower bound as a linear bound in the GapHD input length. No lossless gadget replacement was found; XOR's constant-size gadget is precisely where the lifting proof fails.

## Checkpoint — Unix 1787867838

- **Verified density-jump lemma** — Let `q_d` be the fraction of pairs at Hamming distance `d` labeled `1` by a completion. If for central same-parity layers `|q_{d+2}-q_d| >= epsilon`, then `R(f)=Omega(epsilon^2 n / log(1/epsilon))`; in particular, any constant jump gives the desired linear bound. To prove this, reduce fixed-density Unique Disjointness to distances `d` and `d+2` using the padding construction already verified. Apply a public uniform translation and coordinate permutation; conditional on either distance, the resulting ordered pair is uniform on that distance layer. Amplify the alleged protocol's pointwise error below `epsilon/4` using `O(log(1/epsilon))` repetitions, and estimate its mean using `O(1/epsilon^2)` trials. The two UDISJ cases then have a constant distinguishable mean gap, so the total communication is `O(C log(1/epsilon)/epsilon^2)` and must be `Omega(n)`. The unresolved case is exactly a diffuse profile with every same-parity layer-density increment small; dictator-type middle colorings show that such diffuse profiles really occur.
- **Attempt 26: transcript signatures on diagonal and anti-diagonal** — For a fixed deterministic seed, pair the rejecting leaf reached by `(x,x)` with the accepting leaf reached by `(x,bar(x))`. ## Checkpoint — Unix 1787869407

- **Verified failure of weighted coordinate voting (rounding corrections at Unix 1787871006 and 1787871119)** — If a protocol samples coordinate `i` with weight `w_i` and outputs the mismatch bit, then `p(S)=sum_{i in S}w_i`. Correctness makes every low-cardinality subset have weight at most `1/3` and every high-cardinality subset have weight at least `2/3`. When `3` divides `n`, the largest-third condition indeed forces all weights uniform. For arbitrary `n`, exact uniformity need not follow, so the earlier wording was too strong. Here is the correct subset-sum proof for `n>=6`. Every singleton has weight at most `1/3`. If no subset sum lies in `(1/3,2/3)`, choose an inclusion-minimal subset `S` of weight at least `2/3`. For every `i in S`, minimality gives `w(S)-w_i<=1/3`, hence `w_i>=w(S)-1/3>=1/3`. Thus `S` consists of exactly two atoms of weight `1/3`; otherwise its weight exceeds `1` or it is not minimal. The remaining mass `1/3` cannot split into two positive atoms, since adjoining either to one of the first atoms produces a subset sum in `(1/3,2/3)`. Hence there are exactly three nonzero weights, all `1/3`. But a low-cardinality set can contain two of them because `floor(n/3)>=2`, giving acceptance `2/3`, a contradiction. Consequently some subset has acceptance in the forbidden open interval; tail correctness itself ensures that subset has middle cardinality. Thus no weighted coordinate sampler gives a stable total completion. Nonlinear interactive rectangle protocols remain outside this argument.
