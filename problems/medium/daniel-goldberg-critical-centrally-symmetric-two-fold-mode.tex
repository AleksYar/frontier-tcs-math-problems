\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries The Critical Centrally Symmetric Two-Fold Mode}
\end{center}

\section{Definitions}

Let $N$ be the inward normal and let $k>0$ on the unit circle. Consider
the length-normalized critical curve flow
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\gamma
  =
  -\left(k_{ss}+\frac{3}{2}k^3\right)N
  +\lambda(t)\gamma,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \lambda(t)
  =
  \frac{1}{2\pi}
  \left(
    \int_\gamma k_s^2\,ds
    -
    \frac{3}{2}\int_\gamma k^4\,ds
  \right).
\end{equation}
The flow is restricted to smooth embedded curves of length $2\pi$ that are
centrally symmetric about the origin:
\begin{equation}
  \gamma(\theta+\pi)=-\gamma(\theta).
\end{equation}
Assume that the initial curve is a sufficiently small $H^4$ normal graph over
the unit circle.

\section{Best Known Result}

Length normalization removes the scaling mode, and central symmetry removes
the translation modes. The linearized operator damps all remaining modes
except
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{span}\{\cos(2\theta),\sin(2\theta)\}.
\end{equation}
On a local center manifold, the full $O(2)$ symmetry forces the corresponding
complex amplitude to satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \dot A=A\,\Phi(|A|^2),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi$ is real-valued. Thus the local classification reduces to the
first nonzero coefficient in the Taylor expansion of $\Phi$. The source does
not compute this coefficient; it only indicates that this scalar computation,
together with a center-manifold theorem, should decide the classification.

\section{Problem Formalization}


Determine the complete local dynamics of the unit circle.

Derive the first nonzero term in the center-manifold amplitude equation for
the Fourier $n=2$ mode and decide whether the circle is:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item asymptotically stable, and if so determine the leading decay law;
  \item Lyapunov unstable; or
  \item contained in a local branch of noncircular normalized equilibria.
\end{enumerate}

Lift the normal-form conclusion to the geometric flow modulo
reparametrization.
