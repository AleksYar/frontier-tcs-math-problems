\section{Preliminaries}

\begin{definition}[Congested Clique]
An input is a simple undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ with $|V|=n$. There is one
processor for each vertex of $G$. The processors have distinct
$O(\!\log n)$-bit identifiers and communicate over the complete graph on
$V$ in synchronous rounds. In each round, every ordered pair of processors
can exchange an $O(\!\log n)$-bit message. Initially, every processor
knows $n$ and all processor identifiers, and processor $v$ knows all edges of
$G$ incident to $v$. Local computation is unrestricted.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Triangle detection]
A triangle in $G$ consists of three distinct vertices $u,v,w$ such that
$uv,vw,wu\in E$. An algorithm detects triangles if all processors output
$0$ whenever $G$ is triangle-free, while at least one processor outputs
$1$ whenever $G$ contains a triangle.

A randomized $T(n)$-round algorithm must terminate within $T(n)$ rounds on
every execution and satisfy the preceding condition with probability at
least $2/3$ on every input graph and every assignment of identifiers.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Matrix-multiplication exponent]
Let $M(N)$ be the minimum number of arithmetic operations over $\mathbb{Q}$
needed to multiply two matrices in $\mathbb{Q}^{N\times N}$. The
\emph{matrix-multiplication exponent} is
\[
\omega:=\inf\bigl\{\tau\geq 0:M(N)=O(N^\tau)\bigr\}.
\]
Consequently, $M(N)=O(N^{\omega+\delta})$ for every constant $\delta>0$.
It is known that $2\leq\omega<3$, and it is open whether $\omega=2$.
\end{definition}

The matrix-multiplication approach gives triangle detection in
$n^{1-2/\omega+o(1)}$ Congested Clique rounds.

\section{Problem formulation}

Prove that there exist constants $\varepsilon>0$ and $C\geq 0$, and a
possibly randomized Congested Clique algorithm with error at most $1/3$,
that detects triangles in
\[
O\!\left(
 n^{\max\{0,\,1-2/\omega-\varepsilon\}}(\log n)^C
\right)
\]
rounds. For $\omega>2$, this is a fixed polynomial improvement over the
exponent $1-2/\omega$; for $\omega=2$, the requested bound is
polylogarithmic.
